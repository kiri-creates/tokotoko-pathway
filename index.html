🛡️ Relationship Pathway 1: Roles & Responsibilities
🛶

Roles & Responsibilities: Anchoring Your Whānau

In our Māori and Pacific cultures, as in all healthy communities, a man's role isn't about dominance—it is about shelter, service, moral courage, and providing an emotional and physical anchor for the whānau. When you embrace your responsibilities realistically, pride replaces guilt.

Featured Watch & Reflection

Understanding Our Stories & The People in Our Care

Watch this powerful perspective on understanding the human beings around you beyond surface-level assumptions. How we view our loved ones defines how faithfully we serve and protect them.

Watch Video on YouTube

⚙️ Tools to Strengthen & Position Yourself for Your Roles

TOOL 1
Role Clarification Check

Sit down and write what your partner, kids, or parents truly need from you right now. Often it is not money; it is your calm presence, dependability, and safety.

TOOL 2
The Capacity Filter

Do not make promises you cannot keep. Living up to your role means saying "yes" to 2 core commitments you will never fail, rather than promising 10 and dropping 8.

TOOL 3
Whānau Check-in Talanoa

A simple weekly 15-minute conversation with your household: "How are we doing as a team? What do you need from me this week?"

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ Own One Household Burden: Pick one responsibility (school drop-off, dinner prep, taking the rubbish or finances) and execute it 100% without needing to be asked or thanked.
  • ✓ Be the Tone Setter: Notice when tension enters your home. Rather than reacting or shouting, take a breath, drop your shoulders, and speak with calm authority.
  • ✓ Community Service Stance: Offer an hour to your marae, your local sports club, or elderly neighbor to reinforce your role as an active protector in your rohe.

👁️ Relationship Pathway 2: Being Present
❤️

Your Attention & Affection: The Greatest Gift

You can be sitting in the same lounge with your partner and kids, yet be a million miles away in your head or scrolling on your phone. To a child or a partner, attention equals love. Being truly present means giving your eyes, ears, and emotional focus fully to them.

What Real Presence Looks Like in Everyday Life

❌ Distracted "Half-Present" Man:

Staring at the screen while answering with "uh-huh", quick to lose patience, body in the room but thoughts stressed about yesterday or tomorrow.

✅ Anchored "Fully-Present" Man:

Phone placed face down in another room. Eye contact made when speaking. Kneeling down to child's eye level. Listening without rushing to fix or defend.

🧰 Tools to Help You Stay Grounded & Present

TOOL 1
The 3-Second Threshold Reset

Before you open the front door when you return home, pause outside for 3 seconds. Take a deep breath, leave the workday or worries outside, and walk in ready to embrace your whānau.

TOOL 2
The Device Basket

Put your phone into a designated box or basket for the first 60 minutes after you arrive home or during dinner. Physical barriers prevent unconscious checking.

TOOL 3
Child-Led Play / Partner Date

Surrender the agenda: let your tamariki or partner pick an activity without negotiating or changing it, and join in with wholehearted enthusiasm.

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ The Week 4 Pillar Challenge: Ask your partner or child: "What would you like the two of us to do together today?" and do it completely uninterrupted.
  • ✓ 20-Second Hug: Give your partner or children a warm, genuine 20-second hug. Neuroscience shows this floods the nervous system with oxytocin and restores felt security.
  • ✓ Eye-Contact Check: Practice looking directly into the eyes of whānau members when they speak, acknowledging their words before responding.

🛡️ Relationship Pathway 3: Belonging & Brotherhood
👥

Belonging: Building Your Brotherhood

"Show me your friends, and I will show you your future." An isolated man is vulnerable to old habits, depression, and frustration. When you surround yourself with men who hold high standards, tell you the truth, and lift you up, you build an unbreakable vaka.

The Two Types of Circles: Which Are You Feeding?

Making conscious decisions about your peer group is not about abandoning people—it is about choosing who earns access to influence your life.

⚠️ The Convenient Drinking / Complaining Circle:

Bonded over excuses, substance use, criticising others, or keeping you comfortable in unemployment and low motivation. Pulls you backward.

🌿 The Tokotoko Brotherhood:

Bonded over physical vitality, whānau commitment, honest talanoa, career goals, and healthy accountability. Pulls you upward into your best self.

⚓ Tools to Build & Protect Your Sense of Belonging

TOOL 1
The 5-Brother Audit

Take an honest look at the five people you talk with most. Are they working, exercising, speaking life into their families, or are they dragging you down?

TOOL 2
The Low-Pressure Check-in Call

You don't need a crisis to reach out. A 60-second message: "Thinking of you bro, hope you and the whānau are good" strengthens brotherhood immovably.

TOOL 3
Shared Shoulder-to-Shoulder Mahi

Men talk best when looking at the same task rather than looking at each other. Fishing, moving timber, fitness, and our Friday walking group build natural connection.

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ Building My Brotherhood Log: Reach out to one brother from the Tokotoko group or an old, trustworthy friend this week and check in on how he is tracking.
  • ✓ Participate in Friday Connection: Show up for our walking group or our upcoming Fishing Day activity—experience brotherhood in motion.
  • ✓ Set a Gentle Boundary: Say "no thanks" to a gathering or person that regularly tempts you into old vices or unproductive drama.

🌊 Spiritual Pathway 3: Community & Kaitiakitanga
🌿

Millan Ruka (MNZM): Kaitiaki of Te Tai Tokerau

Millan Ruka (MNZM) is a deeply respected Northland environmental champion, kaitiaki (guardian), and digital creator based right here in Whangārei. A descendant of Ngāpuhi (Te Uriroroi, Te Parawhau, and Te Mahurehure ki Whatitiri hapū), he has spent decades protecting our sacred waterways.

🛶 Key Projects & Core Mahi

  • Environment River Patrol – Aotearoa (ERP-A): Founded this independent watchdog group to monitor major Northland rivers (Wairua, Māngere, Mangakahia). From kayaks and a patrol boat, he documented stock damage and agricultural runoff using GPS photography.
  • The Ruka Marine Turbine (RMT): Melding modern technology with mātauranga Māori, spent 25 years designing clean-energy river turbines that generate renewable electricity without disrupting native eel populations.
  • Defending Porotī Springs: Mobilised hundreds of community members to successfully halt a commercial water bottling factory directly adjacent to the sacred, pure waters of Porotī Springs.

🏅 Tangible Impact & Accolades

  • Hundreds of Kilometres of Fencing: Over 700 formal pollution reports forced council regulatory action, keeping cattle out of vital waterways.
  • National Policy & Iwi Mana: Advised on Kāhui Wai Māori (Te Mana o te Wai principles) and helped secure landmark recognition of iwi mana over Porotī Springs.
  • Protecting Taonga Species: Frontline defense for depleting tuna (native longfin eel) habitats.
MNZM 2018 Voice of the River 2016 Kiwibank Local Hero

Why Community Service Heals the Spirit:

Millan Ruka's journey teaches us that real leadership starts right where your feet are. When you step up to protect your local environment or lend a hand to someone else in need, your personal anxiety fades into meaningful collective purpose.
