🌊 Spiritual Pathway 3: Kaitiakitanga & Community Service
🌿

Millan Ruka (MNZM): Whangārei Environmental Kaitiaki

Millan Ruka (MNZM) is a deeply respected Northland environmental champion, kaitiaki (guardian), and digital creator based right here in Whangārei. A descendant of Ngāpuhi iwi (Te Uriroroi, Te Parawhau, and Te Mahurehure ki Whatitiri hapū), he has dedicated decades to protecting and restoring fresh waterways across Te Tai Tokerau.

Key Projects & Core Work:

Environment River Patrol (ERP-A)

Founded this watchdog group to monitor Northland's major river systems (Wairua, Māngere, Mangakahia). Operating from kayaks and a river boat, he uses GPS photography and video to relentlessly document stock damage and effluent entering water.

The "Ruka Marine Turbine" (RMT)

Melding modern technology with mātauranga Māori, spent 25 years developing a clean-energy turbine designed to generate renewable electricity from shallow, swift river currents without disrupting local eel (tuna) populations.

Defending Porotī Springs

Led a massive community campaign via ActionStation that mobilised hundreds of people to successfully halt a planned commercial water bottling factory right next to the sacred, pure water source of Porotī Springs.

Tangible Achievements & Recognitions

• Hundreds of Kilometres of Fencing: Filed over 700 highly detailed pollution reports to local councils, forcing regulatory action and hundreds of kilometres of new riparian fencing to keep cattle out of rivers.

• Policy & Water Rights Advocacy: Served on national Kāhui Wai Māori (Freshwater Advisory Group), shaping Te Mana o te Wai principles and contributing to the landmark deal recognising iwi mana over Porotī Springs.

• Conservation of Taonga Species: Frontline work shielding depleting populations of native tuna (longfin eel) from toxic runoff.

• Official Honours: Member of the New Zealand Order of Merit (MNZM in 2018), "Voice of the River" Award (2016 NZ River Awards), and Kiwibank Local Hero Medalist (2013).

👁️ Relationship Pathway 2: Being Present
❤️

Your Attention & Affection: The Greatest Gift

You can be sitting in the same lounge with your partner and kids, yet be a million miles away in your head or scrolling on your phone. To a child or a partner, attention equals love. Physical presence without emotional attention leaves loved ones feeling isolated.

Distracted Presence

Being in the room, but on screens

Eyes glued to TikTok or Facebook, giving half-answers, snapping when interrupted, or thinking about work worries instead of listening.

Undivided Presence

Eyes, Ears & Heart locked in

Eye-level contact with your tamariki, active nodding, putting the device face down, and validating feelings before trying to give solutions.

🧰 Tools to Build Daily Presence

TOOL 1
The 3-Second Threshold Reset

Before stepping through your front door at home, pause for 3 seconds. Exhale work stress. Walk in ready to offer warmth and eye contact.

TOOL 2
The Device Basket

Designate 6:00pm - 7:30pm as no-phone family time. Keep phones charging in another room during dinner and bedtime routines.

TOOL 3
Child-Led Play / Partner Date

Let your child choose what to do for 30 minutes without directing them. Or take your partner for an evening stroll along the Whangārei loop.

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ The 20-Second Hug: Greet your partner or tamariki with a genuine, uninterrupted 20-second hug. It releases oxytocin and signals physical safety.
  • ✓ Child’s Choice Activity: Complete this week’s habit milestone: let your tamariki or partner pick an activity to do together with zero phones allowed.

👑 Leadership Pathway 1: Strong Male Leaders
🎓

Tāne Marsters: From Teen Dad to Classroom Kaiako

Becoming a father at just 16 years old could have easily derailed Tāne Marsters (Cook Islands Māori, Ngāpuhi). Instead, he made his child his ultimate reason to step up. Rather than quitting school, he persevered to become Head Student, graduated from the University of Auckland with a Bachelor of Education, and became an inspiring kaiako (teacher).

Featured Message for Young Parents

"My child was never an excuse to stop; she was the reason I had to succeed."

Tāne's message to any man facing early fatherhood or past setbacks is clear: Leadership is not about never making mistakes. It is about taking full ownership of your next chapter. When you show your tamariki that adversity cannot stop you from learning and serving, they will carry that resilience forever.

⚙️ Tools to Identify & Develop Leadership Skills

TOOL 1
Lead by Demonstration

People do not follow what you say; they copy how you behave under pressure. Lead by keeping your word, even on tiny daily tasks.

TOOL 2
The Ownership Switch

Eradicate finger-pointing. When a challenge arises at home or work, ask: "What part of this do I own, and how can I fix it?"

TOOL 3
Continuous Education

Like Tāne, never stop learning. Upskilling in a trade, learning te reo Māori, or reading builds generational respect.

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ The Leadership Commitment: Choose one area in your household where you have hesitated (finances, routines, mornings) and take positive, calm charge of it this week.
  • ✓ Encourage Another Dad: Reach out to a young or struggling father in your circle and remind him that his tamariki are looking up to him.

🏠 Communication Pathway 2: Personal Settings
🗣️

Personal Settings: Home, Friends & Whānau

At home and with our mates, our default communication is often shaped by old habits: shutting down, raising our voices, or defensiveness. Positive communication at home means replacing quick reactions with curiosity and calm clarity.

🧰 Tools for Personal Settings

TOOL 1
The 5-Second Pause

When you feel triggered or criticised, wait 5 seconds before speaking. Drop your shoulders and take a slow breath to allow logic to overtake adrenaline.

TOOL 2
Reflective Paraphrasing

Before offering a solution, say: "What I hear you saying is that you feel overwhelmed. Is that right?" This defuses arguments instantly.

TOOL 3
"I" Statements Over "You" Blame

Replace "You never help me" with "I am feeling stressed and would really appreciate a hand with dinner."

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ The Unconditional Apology: If you reacted poorly or raised your voice, approach your whānau and say: "I apologize for how I handled that. You deserved calm words."
  • ✓ Express Gratitude Out Loud: Thank your partner, brother, or children for one specific thing they did today.

💼 Communication Pathway 3: Professional Settings
🤝

Professional Settings: Workplace & Employer Ready

In job interviews, toolbox talks, and team environments, clear professional communication is your greatest asset. It builds credibility, proves reliability, and ensures employers see you as a dependable long-term hire.

🛠️ Tools for Professional Communication

TOOL 1
Eye Contact & Solid Handshake

Square your shoulders, look the employer in the eye, and speak with steady volume. Non-verbal confidence speaks before you say a single word.

TOOL 2
The 3-Point Answer Framework

When asked a question: 1. Direct answer. 2. A real-world example from your experience. 3. What you learned from it. Avoid rambling.

TOOL 3
Clarify Instructions Immediately

Never nod along if you do not understand a site job. Say: "Just to confirm I do this right the first time, you want X completed by 2pm?"

Practical Application This Week:

  • ✓ The 30-Second Elevator Pitch: Practice introducing yourself, your work history, and your goals to a Tokotoko brother or mirror.
  • ✓ Phone Professionalism: Answer calls this week with a clear, polite greeting ("Kia ora, this is Rawiri speaking") rather than a casual "yeah?"
